About

I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Computer Science and Engineering at Amrita Vishwa Vidyapeetham, Amritapuri.

My PhD thesis is titled Thermal Image Synthesis and Refinement through Generative Models with Mixed-Pixel Analysis and Correction. Dr. Sunitha E. V. and Dr. Jyothisha J. Nair supervised it. It asks two questions. How do we generate convincing thermal images when data are scarce? And how do we find and repair pixels whose readings mix two surfaces?

Since 2015 I have published 38 papers indexed in Scopus. Most of them cover thermal imaging and applied machine learning.

Research

Thermal image synthesis

Thermal datasets are small. Cameras are costly, field work is slow and clinical images are hard to share. I build generative models that produce realistic thermal images from few samples.

LiquidGAN pairs a Neural ODE generator with a liquid neural network discriminator. An RK4 solver refines the latent representation, and the pair models the temperature-dependent structure of a scene. Earlier, I used DCGAN variants to augment neonatal infrared datasets and Pix2Pix to detect unmanned aerial vehicles.

  • 2024Respiratory syndromes in DCGAN-augmented neonatal infrared datasets
  • 2022Detecting unmanned aerial vehicles with Pix2Pix

Mixed pixels

A pixel that straddles a leaf and the soil behind it reports a temperature that belongs to neither. Every measurement built on that value inherits the error. I work on finding these pixels and repairing them.

My 2026 paper in Smart Agricultural Technology uses an unsupervised convolutional autoencoder to flag mixed pixels and a local, context-based step to correct them. I tested the method on neonatal and agricultural imagery. With Fathima Mariya A. K., I have compared thresholding and Gaussian mixture methods, and built a framework that detects, unmixes and reconstructs.

  • 2026Autoencoder detection with local correction, Smart Agricultural Technology
  • 2026Detection, unmixing and reconstruction, Procedia Computer Science
  • 2026Thresholding and GMM methods compared

Thermal sensing for agriculture

A plant that has water cools itself. A plant that does not, heats up. Thermal cameras read that signal. I use it to estimate the crop water stress index (CWSI) on chilli leaves and to rebuild low-resolution thermal frames. I also classify chilli leaf disease from thermal images, and rice leaf disease from thermal and RGB images together. Related work detects bruises in sapota fruit and predicts shelf life.

  • 2026Rice leaf disease with ConvNeXt on thermal and RGB images
  • 2025Segmenting sunlit chilli leaves for CWSI
  • 2025Chilli leaf disease detection
  • 2024Low-resolution thermal reconstruction for CWSI

Thermal sensing for health and behaviour

Skin temperature carries physiological signals. My earlier papers read them: respiratory syndromes in infants, heart rate and blood pressure from facial thermal images, facial expression and emotion, driver sobriety and deception.

Teaching and supervision

23CSE201 Procedural Programming in C

I teach this course to third-semester Computer Science and Engineering students. Lab sheets are pseudocode-focused and run in order: functions and storage classes, then one-dimensional arrays and strings, then two-dimensional arrays.

Recursion gets extra time. The practice sheets carry outputs I have checked on a compiler, and they build up to multi-branch recursion. For programming contests I run a DOMjudge server on the campus intranet. It judges submissions from about 150 students.

Projects with students

I supervise undergraduate and postgraduate projects on thermal imaging and applied machine learning. Current open topics:
